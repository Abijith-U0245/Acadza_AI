# Product notes

Students lose the most when a broken high-frequency question teaches a wrong mental model and then compounds the damage through revision and mock tests. The console therefore prioritises high-confidence content defects, groups corroborating reports by question, and makes the evidence visible before a faculty member acts.

I would never let AI auto-apply a new diagram, distractor, chapter assignment, syllabus decision, or wholesale replacement of a question/solution. Those are authored decisions with a high blast radius. Even scoped auto proposals remain unpublished until faculty approval and every action is recorded.

The week-one metric I would watch is **precision of faculty-approved proposals**, split by repair type. A high approval rate with low reversal rate is more useful than raw automation volume.

Beyond this brief, I would pitch a “shadow cohort” check: after a correction is approved, compare answer-choice distributions and solve time before/after to detect whether the fix actually removes student confusion without introducing a new one.
